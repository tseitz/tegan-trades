# Phase 3.3 — Backtesting / Roster Scoring

## Design

**Goal:** grade each roster feed's distilled calls against a price oracle and produce a
*defensible* per-person score that can drive add/drop decisions.

**Load-bearing principle (inherited from 3a/3b):** `data/theses/` is regenerable ore. Grades
are a **read-time lens plus a price cache** — never a write-back into the firehose. `core.grade`
and `core.score` are pure (duck-typed inputs, no I/O), exactly like `canon.resolve` and
`rank.score`; `packages/oracle/` owns all network + cache I/O.

**Attribution is feed-level for this phase.** `Technical Roundup (CryptoCred + DonAlt)` and
`1000x Podcast` are scored as units. Accepted deliberately — every report must label these rows
as multi-author so a score is never misread as one person's.

### Settled decisions (this session)

**1. Grading is staged.**
- **Tier 1 — direction + horizon (3.3a).** Grades all 3704 theses. Entry = close on
  `published_at`, exit = close at `published_at + horizon(timeframe)`. This is the only thing
  gradeable on the corpus as it stands, and the only thing that works for the 2546 `macro_lean`
  theses (69% of corpus, mostly empty `key_levels`).
- **Tier 2 — level-aware (3.3b, gated on 3.3a landing).** `key_levels` is an *unlabeled bag of
  floats* — real example, HYPE long: `[40, 50, 30, 21, 19, 20]` where 40 is a trigger, 50 a
  target, the rest downside. `invalidation` is free text with conditions baked in ("Daily closes
  above 2400"). Nothing mechanical separates target from stop, so Tier 2 requires an LLM
  enrichment pass over the 1158 trade theses.

**2. Score = matched-slate edge, not raw hit rate.** 65% of all calls are `long` over a rising
two-year window, so raw hit rate would rank everyone highly and separate nobody. Every person is
compared against a **null model evaluated on their own exact slate** of `(asset, publish_date,
horizon)` tuples — "what would always-long have returned on precisely these calls?" That
controls for regime and asset mix. Report both:
- `hit_rate` and `null_hit_rate` (intuitive; the sanity check that the grader isn't lying)
- `mean_signed_return − mean_null_return` ← **the headline "edge" number**

**3. Oracle routing is curated-first, and must never guess.** The naive "try Coinbase, fall back
to Yahoo" design is actively dangerous here: Coinbase lists a token with symbol `SPX`
(SPX6900), and the corpus has **204 SPX theses** meaning the S&P 500. Same collision on `META`
(11) and `IP` (8). Routing on the `domain` field alone is *also* insufficient — 2 SPX theses are
tagged `crypto`, plus 16 GOLD-crypto and 1 MSTR-crypto. So:
> `cfg/oracle_map.yaml` (committed, curated) is the source of truth. `domain` is a *validator*,
> not a router. Anything unmapped and ambiguous resolves to `unpriceable` and is logged for
> curation — never silently priced.

**4. Pending is a first-class state.** Only 72% of theses have cleared their horizon
(`scalp` 97%, `swing` 92%, `position` 43%, `macro` 24%). Unresolved theses are `pending`,
excluded from scores, and counted in every report. Never silently dropped.

**5. Restatements are folded before scoring.** Reuse `triage_cli.collapse_restatements` — a
person who repeats one BTC-long 20× must not get that view weighted 20×. Report folded as
primary, unfolded as secondary.

### Oracle sources (all probed and verified this session)

| Source | Role | Verified |
|---|---|---|
| **Coinbase Exchange** `/products/{SYM}-USD/candles` | crypto primary | Free, keyless, full 2y history, 402 online USD pairs. 300 candles/req → ~3 paginated calls per symbol. |
| **Yahoo** `v8/finance/chart/{sym}` | stock/macro | Free, keyless. Validated: TSLA, HOOD, MSTR, NVDA, GOOGL, MU, INTC, COIN, PLTR, ORCL, UUUU, URA, REMX, XLE, SPY, TLT, `^GSPC`, `^NDX`, `DX-Y.NYB`, `GC=F`, `SI=F`, `HG=F`, `CL=F`, `YM=F`, `EURUSD=X`. Returns `meta.instrumentType` (EQUITY/ETF/FUTURE/CURRENCY/INDEX) — use it to validate bare tickers. |
| **Kraken** `/0/public/OHLC` | crypto fallback only | Rescues **70 theses / 19 assets** Coinbase lacks (XMR 22, TRX 9, MEME 7, GRASS 6…). **Hard 720-candle cap** — returns from 2024-08-03, already 3 days short of corpus start, and the window slides forward daily. Fallback only; cache aggressively before it ages out. |

**Rejected:** *Binance* — geo-blocked (HTTP 451). *CoinGecko* — free tier hard-caps history at
365 days (`error_code: 10012`), cannot cover the 2y corpus; keep it only for the existing
`tickers.json` rank snapshot. *stooq* — sits behind a JS proof-of-work wall, not machine-readable.
*TradingView* — **has no official public market-data API, and a paid Pro subscription does not
grant one**; its three APIs (Charting Library, Datafeed, Broker REST) all require *you* to supply
the data. The "TradingView API" vendors are unaffiliated scrapers with ToS/redistribution risk.
*On-chain oracles (Chainlink/Pyth)* — built for trustless on-chain settlement, not backtesting;
worse long-tail coverage than Coinbase and more infrastructure. They'd only win for DEX-only
tokens that never hit a CEX, and this corpus's unpriced tail is **equities** (TSLA, HOOD, MSTR,
NVDA, GOOGL…), not DEX tokens — so they solve a problem we don't have.

**Resulting coverage:** ~95%+ of the corpus priceable. Genuinely unpriceable and explicitly
marked: `__basket__` (49), `BTC.D` (40), `ALTS` (29), `CRYPTO` (16), `TOTAL3` (13), `SPACEX` (14,
private), `FEDFUNDS` (6). `ETH/BTC` (24) is **derived** from the two legs, not fetched.

## Patterns to Mirror

- `packages/core/src/core/rank.py` — the exact shape for `grade.py`/`score.py`: pure module,
  `@dataclass(frozen=True)` weights, duck-typed `Protocol` inputs (`_ThesisLike`, `_SourceLike`),
  corpus context passed in by the caller, no I/O anywhere in the scoring path.
- `packages/core/src/core/canon.py` — `resolve()` as a non-mutating lens + `load_registry()` as
  the segregated I/O boundary. `oracle/route.py` mirrors this split.
- `packages/core/src/core/rank.py:parse_date` / `corpus_span` — the tolerant-degradation idiom
  (malformed/missing dates return `None` and degrade, never raise). Price gaps must behave the
  same way.
- `packages/distill/src/distill/store.py` — `DATA_ROOT` resolution via `parents[N]`, JSON
  read/write shape. `oracle/cache.py` copies this for `data/prices/`.
- `packages/distill/src/distill/triage_cli.py` — `collapse_restatements` (reuse directly);
  `rank_corpus` corpus-walk; `input_fn=input, out=print` injection for testable CLIs.
- `packages/distill/src/distill/canon_cli.py` — CLI arg/subcommand conventions.

## Tasks

### 1. `packages/oracle/` — new package, price I/O (TDD, fixtures not live calls)
- `sources/coinbase.py`, `sources/yahoo.py`, `sources/kraken.py`. Each exposes
  `fetch_daily(symbol, start, end) -> list[Bar]`. Handle Coinbase's 300-candle pagination and
  Kraken's 720 cap explicitly (the cap is a documented, tested constraint, not a surprise).
- `series.py`: `PriceSeries` frozen dataclass; `close_on(d)` with a **forward-fill policy for
  non-trading days** (weekends/holidays hit equities constantly) and a configurable max
  forward-fill gap beyond which it returns `None` rather than inventing a price.
- `cache.py`: `data/prices/{source}/{symbol}.json`, incrementally extendable, gitignored ore.
- Tests use recorded JSON fixtures — the suite must never hit the network.

### 2. `cfg/oracle_map.yaml` + `oracle/route.py` — curated routing with a collision guard
- Curated map covering the top ~80 canonical assets (≈85%+ of corpus) → `{source, symbol}`.
- `route(asset_canonical, domain) -> OracleRef | Unpriceable`. Curated map wins. For the
  unmapped tail: `crypto` → Coinbase then Kraken; `stock`/`macro` → Yahoo validated via
  `instrumentType`.
- **Collision guard:** refuse to auto-route to a crypto source when the majority of an asset's
  theses are `stock`/`macro` domain (and vice versa) — emit a conflict for curation instead of
  guessing. Regression-test `SPX`, `META`, `IP` explicitly.
- `unpriceable` reasons are enumerated (`basket`, `dominance_metric`, `private_company`,
  `rate`, `unmapped`) so reports can break coverage down honestly.

### 3. `fetch-prices` CLI — backfill the cache
- Walks the corpus, routes every distinct asset, fetches and caches the needed date span
  (corpus min → today + max horizon). Resumable, rate-limit aware, `--dry-run` to print the
  routing table and coverage before spending calls.
- Emits an unpriceable/conflict report → drives the first `oracle_map.yaml` curation pass.

### 4. `packages/core/src/core/grade.py` — pure Tier-1 grading (TDD)
- `HORIZONS = {scalp: 7, swing: 30, position: 180, macro: 365}` as a tunable frozen dataclass
  (v1 best-guess, same convention as `RankWeights`).
- `grade(thesis, series, *, today, horizons) -> Grade | Pending | Unpriceable` — a sum type, so
  "no score" is never confused with "score of zero".
- `Grade` carries `entry`, `exit`, `realized_return`, `signed_return`, `correct`, plus the
  `null_return` (always-long on the same slate) for downstream matched comparison.
- `neutral` direction: correct when `|realized| < flat_band` (configurable, default ~2%).
- Entry convention: close of `published_at` — the call was made at/before publish, so entering
  at that close is conservative and leak-free. Document it.

### 5. `packages/core/src/core/score.py` — pure aggregation (TDD)
- `score_person(grades) -> PersonScore`: `n`, `n_pending`, `n_unpriceable`, `hit_rate`,
  `null_hit_rate`, `mean_signed_return`, `mean_null_return`, **`edge`**, and a
  **bootstrap confidence interval** on `edge`.
- **Sample-size guard:** below a threshold (default n=30) return the score flagged
  `insufficient_sample` and refuse to rank. Per-person resolved counts today range 57–610.
- Per-person **per-asset breakdown** — BTC is 1056 theses corpus-wide; without this a score is
  just a BTC opinion. Also break out by `timeframe` and `direction` (long vs short skill differ).

### 6. `score-roster` CLI — the report
- Ranked table: person, n, edge ± CI, hit rate vs null, coverage %, `[multi-author]` tag.
- `--by-asset` / `--by-timeframe` / `--by-direction` drills.
- `--fold/--no-fold` toggle for restatement collapsing (default fold).
- Writes a dated summary the vault can absorb; **does not** mutate `data/theses/`.

### 7. *(3.3b — gated on 1–6 landing)* Tier-2 level-aware grading
- LLM enrichment pass over the 1158 trade theses → typed `{entry, targets[], stop, condition}`
  where `condition ∈ {touch, daily_close, weekly_close}`.
- Stored as a **sidecar keyed by thesis `id`** (`data/grades/levels.jsonl`), mirroring the 3b
  triage-decisions sidecar. Content-addressed ids (from `abe46ac`) mean unchanged theses keep
  their enrichment across a re-distill.
- Grading becomes path-dependent: did price reach a target before the stop, within the horizon?
  → R-multiple, which is a far better input to `edge` than a fixed-horizon return.

## Outcome (3.3a shipped 2026-07-24)

Tasks 1–6 landed. 163 new tests, all green. Coverage: **91.1% of the corpus priceable**
(Coinbase 59.7% · Yahoo 29.7% · Kraken 1.7%); the remaining 8.9% is explicitly categorized,
never silently mis-priced.

**Corrections to assumptions in the Design section above, found during implementation:**

1. **The base-rate problem runs the opposite way.** The design assumed a rising window would
   inflate hit rates. It doesn't — BTC ran 65k → 105k then collapsed to ~60k by Jul 2026, so
   mean market return across graded calls is **−1.11%** and every hit rate lands under 51%.
   The null-model correction is still the right fix; the direction of the bias was wrong.
2. **Yahoo's `instrumentType` is not sufficient validation.** Bare `GOLD` resolves to
   *Gold.com, Inc.* (a real EQUITY), `CL` to Colgate-Palmolive, `OIL` to an iPath ETN, `SPX`
   and `XAU` to mutual funds. Type-checking can't catch a real-but-wrong instrument, so the
   curated map is mandatory, backed by a structural "is this even ticker-shaped" guard.
3. **`direction_edge` is structurally zero for long-only callers** — being long *is* the
   always-long null. On a 65%-long corpus that metric alone would flatten the roster, which
   is why `benchmark_edge` (vs BTC / S&P 500) became the headline instead.
4. **Benchmarks must span the whole corpus, not their own mentions.** ^GSPC was first
   *mentioned* 2024-09-25 while the corpus opens 2024-07-31, so windowing it like an ordinary
   asset silently stripped the benchmark off every earlier stock/macro call. Fixed in
   `plan_fetches` + `oracle/benchmarks.py`.

**The result that matters: the ranking is not yet trustworthy.** `--horizon-sweep` reports
**top-3 CHURNS** — Benjamin Cowen moves from 8th to 1st when horizons are doubled. And 8 of 9
feeds have a benchmark-edge CI straddling zero. Only Material Indicators is statistically
distinguishable (+7.2%, CI [+0.7%, +13.9%], n=65), and its entire edge is shorts (29 calls,
65.5% hit, +16.1%) while its longs are flat. Treat the table as instrumentation, not a verdict.

## Considerations / open questions

- **Horizon values are guesses.** 7/30/180/365 are unvalidated. Once Tier 1 runs, sweep horizons
  and check whether rankings are stable across them — if a person's edge flips sign between a
  30d and 60d swing horizon, the score is horizon-fitting, not skill. Worth a `--horizon-sweep`.
- **`position`/`macro` samples are thin** (43%/24% resolved). Long-horizon skill will not be
  measurable this cycle; report it as pending rather than scoring it noisily.
- **Kraken's window is closing.** Its 720-day cap already excludes pre-2024-08-03 data. Cache
  those 70 theses' assets early or lose them permanently.
- **Multi-author feeds** cap the resolution of this whole phase — 1424 theses (38% of corpus)
  are attributed to two-person feeds. The per-author split is deliberately out of scope here.
- **Sandbox/network:** the settings allowlist names only `api.coingecko.com`, but
  `api.exchange.coinbase.com`, `query1.finance.yahoo.com`, and `api.kraken.com` all resolved
  fine in probing. Add them to `.claude/settings.json` proactively if a fetch ever gets blocked.
- **Survivorship in the roster itself** — we only ingest people currently on the watchlist. The
  scores answer "who among these is good", not "who is good".
